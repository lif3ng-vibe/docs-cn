[The Prompt Report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06608) is an extraordinary piece of work. It's a meta-analysis of the entire literature of prompt engineering. The Latent Space crew mentioned it as one of their [top reads for 2025](https://x.com/latentspacepod/status/1872719928618565646).

The thing I love most about it is its careful taxonomy of different terms:

```txt
📁 Prompting Techniques
├── 📁 Zero-Shot
│   ├── 📄 Emotion Prompting
│   ├── 📄 Role Prompting
│   ├── 📄 Style Prompting
│   └── 📄 Self-Ask
│
├── 📁 Few-Shot
│   ├── 📄 Exemplar Generation
│   ├── 📄 Exemplar Ordering
│   └── 📄 Exemplar Selection
│
├── 📁 Thought Generation
│   └── 📁 Chain of Thought
│       ├── 📁 Zero-Shot CoT
│       │   ├── 📄 Analogical Prompting
│       │   ├── 📄 Step-Back Prompting
│       │   └── 📄 Thread-of-Thought
│       │
│       └── 📁 Few-Shot CoT
│           ├── 📄 Active-Prompt
│           ├── 📄 Auto-CoT
│           ├── 📄 Memory-of-Thought
│           └── 📄 Prompt Mining
│
├── 📄 Ensembling
├── 📄 Self-Criticism
└── 📄 Decomposition
```

Yes, this isn't even all of the different techniques mentioned. It even goes into multimodal and multi-lingual techniques, as well as security.

Each section links to further papers. This makes it an ideal place to start if you're looking for more detail in this area.
